Forcing Hyacinths Indoors: Pot Them in Autumn, Bloom Them by Christmas

A hyacinth in flower is one of the strongest scents a small flat can hold. The catch is that hyacinths do not bloom on demand. A bulb needs a cold period first: weeks of cool, dark conditions that convince it winter has happened, so that when warmth and light return it flowers instead of growing a tuft of leaves. Once you understand that, the rest is simple: pot the bulbs in autumn, keep them cool and dark for a couple of months, then bring them into a bright room. Flowers usually follow within two to three weeks; the most common failure is skipping the cold.

The 30-Second Cheat Sheet

  • The cold period is non-negotiable: bulbs need roughly 10 to 13 weeks of cold (around 2–9°C / 35–48°F) in the dark before they flower properly. Skip it and you usually get leaves and no flowers.
  • Plant in autumn: October is the usual month for Christmas blooms.
  • Two ways to grow them: in a pot of compost, or in a hyacinth vase with water touching only the base of the bulb.
  • Cool and bright once they are growing: after the cold period, bring them into a bright room that is cool rather than hot.
  • Water sparingly until growth starts: damp soil or a topped-up vase, not a wet pot.
  • One season of forcing, then done indoors: a forced bulb rarely flowers well indoors a second year.

Why Hyacinths Need the Cold

For hyacinths, the mechanism is simple: the bulb forms its flower inside during the cold period, and the cold tells it winter has passed and spring has arrived. Without a long stretch of cold, the bulb does not complete that preparation, and in warmth it grows leaves and a stunted flower spike — or nothing at all.

That is the main way hyacinths differ from the other forced bulbs here. Paperwhites flower without any cold period — our guide to paperwhites for apartments is built around exactly that convenience — and amaryllis too, as our amaryllis care guide explains. Hyacinths are the one classic winter bulb that asks you to organise a cold period, and the reward is the scent: no other forced bulb smells as strong.

Buy pre-chilled bulbs if you can — many garden centres sell them labelled as prepared, and those flower within a few weeks of a bright room. Do not store bulbs in a fridge with fruit: ripening fruit releases ethylene gas, which damages the flower inside.

Forcing in a Pot of Soil, Step by Step

The pot method is the more forgiving of the two:

  1. Choose a pot with drainage holes — a shallow bowl or a standard pot works, as long as water can escape. Pick the fattest, firmest bulbs you can find: a large bulb gives a larger flower spike. Plant the bulbs close together, almost touching, with each tip just above the soil surface. Wash your hands after handling: hyacinth bulbs can irritate skin.
  2. Fill the pot with ordinary multipurpose compost, set the bulbs on it pointy end up, and fill in around them so they sit firmly with their tips showing. Water lightly, just enough to dampen it.
  3. Give them the cold period: move the pot somewhere cool and dark for roughly 10 to 13 weeks — an unheated spare room, a cool cupboard, a garage, or a fridge shelf. Keep it cool, roughly 2–9°C (35–48°F), and dark.
  4. Check occasionally and water sparingly — the compost should stay just damp. If it dries out completely, give a little water; if it is wet, leave it alone. Wet compost for weeks means rot.
  5. Bring the pot into a cool, bright room once the shoots are a few centimetres tall and you can feel a firm flower bud inside. A cool bright windowsill is ideal — strong heat makes stems stretch and flowers fade fast.
  6. Water a little more once growth is visible, keeping the compost moist but never waterlogged, and turn the pot every few days.

Flowers usually open within two to three weeks of coming into the light, and last about two weeks in a cool room.

Forcing in Water: The Hyacinth Vase

The water method is the cleanest option for a flat, because there is no soil — just a bulb, a vase, and water. Use a proper hyacinth vase, the hourglass-shaped glass that holds the bulb above the water. Fill it so the water just touches the base of the bulb — no higher. The bulb itself should stay dry.

Give it the same cold, dark period — roughly 10 to 13 weeks in a cool dark spot, with the water topped up as it evaporates. Bring it into a cool, bright room once the roots are developed and the shoot is a few centimetres tall. Keep the water topped up and change it if it turns cloudy.

The Calendar: Counting Back From Christmas

Count backwards from the date you want flowers. For Christmas, plant in early to mid-October, give 10 to 13 weeks of cold, and bring the pots into the light in mid-December. Flowers usually follow within two to three weeks — which lands on Christmas week in a cool room. For January flowers, plant in late October or early November. For a succession of blooms, plant three pots a fortnight apart. With pre-chilled bulbs, skip the cold period: flowers usually follow within three to four weeks, so mid-November planting still lands on Christmas.

Caring for the Flowers, and What Happens Afterwards

Once a hyacinth is in flower, keep it cool and upright. In a cool room (around 15–18°C / 59–64°F) it flowers for up to two weeks; in a hot room near a radiator it may finish in a few days. Keep it away from heat, give it bright light but not strong direct sun, and water a little and regularly.

Here is the honest ending: a forced bulb rarely flowers well indoors a second year — it has spent its stored energy on one display. If you have a garden, let the leaves die back naturally, then plant the bulb out in spring — it may flower again outdoors in a year or two. If you have no outdoor space, compost the bulb and buy fresh bulbs next autumn. Do not try to force the same bulb again next winter — the result is usually a tuft of leaves and no flower.

Quick Questions, Straight Answers

How long do hyacinth bulbs need to chill before forcing?

Roughly 10 to 13 weeks of cold, around 2–9°C (35–48°F), in the dark. That is what makes the difference between a proper flower spike and a tuft of leaves.

Can I force hyacinths in water without soil?

Yes — that is what a hyacinth vase is for. Set the bulb in the vase with water just touching its base, give it the same cold dark period, then bring it into a cool bright room.

When should I plant hyacinths for Christmas flowers?

Plant in early to mid-October, give 10 to 13 weeks of cold, and bring the pots into the light in mid-December. Flowers usually follow within two to three weeks.

Why did my forced hyacinth only grow leaves and no flowers?

Almost always because the cold period was too short or skipped. The bulb needs weeks of proper cold to form its flower inside.
